## Tell me about yourself.

Use a **present → past → future** structure in 60–90 seconds:

1. **Present** — who you are now (e.g. "final-year CS student focused on backend development and DSA").
2. **Past** — 2–3 highlights relevant to the role: a project with measurable impact, an internship, a competition result.
3. **Future** — why this role and company is the logical next step.

Keep it professional (no life story), tailored to the job description, and end with a hook they can ask about.

## Why do you want to join our company?

Show you've done your homework: mention something specific about the company's product, engineering culture, tech stack or mission, connect it to your skills and interests, and explain what you'd contribute — not just what you'd gain. Avoid generic answers like "it's a big company" or focusing only on salary.

## What are your strengths?

Pick 2–3 strengths relevant to the role and **prove** each with a short example: "I'm persistent at debugging — during my internship I tracked down a memory leak in our image service that had been open for weeks, cutting its memory use by 40%." Evidence beats adjectives.

## What is your greatest weakness?

Choose a **real but non-critical** weakness, show self-awareness, and describe concrete steps you're taking to improve: "I used to hesitate to ask for help and lose time; now I timebox problems to an hour, then share what I've tried with a teammate." Avoid clichés like "I'm a perfectionist" and avoid weaknesses that are core to the job.

## Where do you see yourself in five years?

Show ambition aligned with the company: growing deeper technical expertise, owning larger systems, mentoring others, possibly leading projects. Keep it realistic and connected to the role — don't say you'll be running your own startup or in a completely different field.

## Describe a challenging project and how you handled it.

Use the **STAR** method:
- **Situation** — the context and why it mattered.
- **Task** — your specific responsibility.
- **Action** — what *you* did (decisions, trade-offs, technical details).
- **Result** — measurable outcome and what you learned.

Prepare 3–4 STAR stories you can adapt: a technical challenge, a conflict, a failure and a leadership moment.

## Tell me about a time you failed.

Pick a genuine failure with real stakes, own your part without blaming others, explain what you learned, and — most importantly — show how you applied that lesson later. Interviewers assess accountability and growth, not perfection.

## How do you handle conflict in a team?

Describe a real example: you listened to understand the other person's perspective, focused on the shared goal and facts (data, user impact) rather than personalities, proposed options or a compromise, escalated respectfully if needed, and kept the working relationship healthy afterwards.

## How do you handle pressure and tight deadlines?

Explain your process with an example: clarify priorities and the definition of done, break work into smaller tasks, communicate early about risks and trade-offs (scope vs time), stay focused on the most important items, and reflect afterwards to prevent the same crunch next time.

## Why should we hire you?

Summarise the match in three parts: the **skills** they need that you have (with proof), the **impact** you've already delivered in similar situations, and the **attitude** you bring (ownership, learning speed, teamwork). Make it specific to the job description and confident without overselling.

## Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?

Answer honestly and positively. If yes, say so clearly. If you have constraints, state them politely with any flexibility you can offer ("I'd prefer Bengaluru, but I'm open to relocating for the right role"). Don't agree to something you can't commit to.

## Do you have any questions for us?

Always ask 2–3 thoughtful questions — it shows genuine interest. Good examples: "What does success look like in the first 90 days?", "How does the team handle code reviews and on-call?", "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing this year?", "How do you support learning and growth?" Avoid asking only about salary or leave in the first conversation.
